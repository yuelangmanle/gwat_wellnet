# Figure captions (submission copy)

**Fig. 1.** Long-term monitoring wells and minimum reliable network sizes. (**a**) Locations of 29,717 eligible wells (1950–2026) across CONUS, colored by trend direction (red, falling, *n* = 18,513; blue, rising, *n* = 11,134; gray, stable, *n* = 70). (**b**) Minimum network size *n*ₘᵢₙ for ≤ 10% sign-reversal risk over 1975–2025: simulated threshold (colored circle), Equation 2 prediction (black diamond), and network size *N* (open circle). Rows grouped by retention (green, ≤ 5%; amber, 5–50%; red, > 50%). Equation 2 reproduces simulated thresholds within a factor of two for 17 of 19 resolvable networks; three weak-signal systems require 88–94% retention.

**Fig. 2.** Sign-reversal probability versus retained network size *n* over 1975–2025. (**a**) Random thinning across the six largest aquifers (shading: < 10%, 10–30%, and > 30% reversal risk; circles: *n*ₘᵢₙ). High-SNR systems remain reliable with few wells (Coastal Lowlands, *n*ₘᵢₙ = 11); Basin and Range reverses frequently at all tested sizes. (**b**) CONUS network: random removal (black curve, 95% binomial band) versus 0.5° clustered removal (orange curve) with Equation 1 (dashed). National trends tolerate random removal down to 297 wells (1%), whereas 7,726 clustered wells (30%) match the reversal risk of ~180–280 random wells.

**Fig. 3.** Sampling law collapse and network design chart. (**a**) Observed sign-reversal probabilities across 216 thinning trials against normalized sample size *c*·SNR·√(*nN*/(*N* − *n*)). Solid curve is Φ(−*x*) with *c* = 1.27 (95% CI: 1.04–1.55) and no fitted shape parameter (SNR = |β_net| / *S*). (**b**) Network design chart showing required retention (*n*ₘᵢₙ / *N*) contours for ≤ 10% reversal risk from Equation 2 across SNR and *N*. Points mark aquifer networks; red shading indicates ≥ 90% retention.

**Figure S1** (Supporting Information). Comparison of deterministic and random well-retention strategies in (**a**) a strong-signal aquifer (High Plains aquifer, *N* = 8,160) and (**b**) a heterogeneous aquifer with offsetting local trends (Central Valley aquifer system, *N* = 183). Curves show absolute slope bias |β̂ − β_net| (m yr⁻¹) versus retained wells *n* under 50 random deletion orders (black curve and gray 95% band), longest-records-first retention (solid red curve), and spatial-spread-first retention (dashed orange curve). In (**a**), longest-records-first retention produces 5.4 times the slope bias of random retention at 10% retention (816 wells). In (**b**), spatial-spread-first retention preserves the trend sign down to 14 wells (8% retention) at a slope bias of up to 3.1 times the reference slope.

Summary: > Virtual thinning of 29,717 long-term U.S. monitoring wells shows that regional groundwater-level trend reliability follows a finite-population sampling law, yielding a closed-form minimum network size n_min = Nz²/(z² + (c·SNR)²N) from two archive statistics (SNR and N) and demonstrating that well reductions must be spatially distributed rather than clustered.
